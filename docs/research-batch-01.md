# Research Batch 01 Notes

## Verified primary and steward sources

### xv6

MIT’s 6.1810 page identifies xv6 as a simple Unix-like teaching operating system developed in summer 2006 and later ported to RISC-V. It links to the maintained source repository and companion text, and connects the project directly to Unix Version 6 source and the Lions commentary. Canonical catalog page: <https://pdos.csail.mit.edu/6.828/2026/xv6.html>.

### Multicians

The Multicians site describes itself as preserving the technical ideas, advances, builders, and users of the Multics operating system. It offers a library of documents and papers, records that Multics began in 1965 and was used until 2000, and points to MIT-hosted open-source material. Canonical catalog page: <https://www.multicians.org/>.

### Computer History Museum releases

CHM’s MacPaint and QuickDraw release makes original MacPaint and QuickDraw source code available with Apple’s permission, and documents MacPaint’s January 1984 release. Its Apple II DOS release contains source listings, source reconstructions, technical documentation, contracts, and meeting minutes around DOS 3.1, released in June 1978. CHM also provides early CP/M source materials from 1975 to 1979 and says that material is for non-commercial use. The Software History Center gives the institutional context for the museum’s historical software, source-code, and oral-history collections.

Primary URLs:

- <https://computerhistory.org/blog/macpaint-and-quickdraw-source-code/>
- <https://computerhistory.org/blog/apple-ii-dos-source-code/>
- <https://computerhistory.org/blog/early-digital-research-cpm-source-code/>
- <https://computerhistory.org/software-history-center/>

### Structure and Interpretation of Computer Programs

MIT Press lists the second edition of *Structure and Interpretation of Computer Programs* by Harold Abelson and Gerald Jay Sussman as published on July 25, 1996. The publisher describes its revised programming-system examples, interpreters and compilers, and coverage of objects with state, concurrency, functional programming, lazy evaluation, nondeterminism, graphics, and stream processing. Canonical publisher page: <https://mitpress.mit.edu/9780262510875/structure-and-interpretation-of-computer-programs/>.

### Virtual AGC

Virtual AGC describes its purpose as making original flight software and emulations of onboard guidance computers from historical spacecraft runnable on modern public computers, with emphasis on the Apollo Guidance Computer flown on lunar missions in the 1960s and 1970s. The site provides archival scans, source-code coverage information, an emulator, and associated documentation. Canonical project page: <https://www.ibiblio.org/apollo/>.

### MS-DOS and early Unix releases

CHM’s MS-DOS source release documents PC DOS 1.0 shipping with the IBM PC in August 1981 and MS-DOS 2.0 shipping with the PC-XT in March 1983. CHM makes the version 1.1 and 2.0 source and object code available for non-commercial use and points readers to Microsoft’s later GitHub release. The CHM early Unix release makes publicly accessible source listings dated to 1970 or early 1971, from the period before Unix migrated from the PDP-7 to the PDP-11. The original material is in PDP-7 assembly and includes early utilities and programs such as `ls`, `rm`, `roff`, `sh`, and *Space Travel*.

Primary URLs:

- <https://computerhistory.org/blog/microsoft-ms-dos-early-source-code/>
- <https://computerhistory.org/blog/the-earliest-unix-code-an-anniversary-source-code-release/>

### Architecture and preservation collections

The Architecture of Open Source Applications presents authors’ explanations of the structure and reasoning of dozens of open-source programs. Its two principal volumes include subjects such as Bash, Berkeley DB, HDFS, LLVM, GDB, GHC, Git, nginx, Open MPI, PyPy, SQLAlchemy, Twisted, and ZeroMQ; the collection is available under Creative Commons Attribution 3.0. Software Heritage states that it collects, preserves, curates, and makes source code accessible as a long-term archive, including an archive browser, source-origin search, APIs, and a legacy-code rescue workflow.

Primary URLs:

- <https://aosabook.org/en/>
- <https://www.softwareheritage.org/>

### The Unix Heritage Society

The Unix Heritage Society’s own landing page identifies the group’s Unix Archive, Unix source-code resources, Heritage Society Services, mailing list, and heritage wiki. Its purpose is the preservation and maintenance of historical Unix artifacts, with source distributions accessible from its archive. Canonical page: <https://www.tuhs.org/>. Access and copyright conditions vary by archived distribution.

### Bitsavers

Bitsavers provides separate archives for software, computing, communications, components, magazines, and test equipment. Its documentation describes a preservation-oriented scanning workflow, states that many documents came from a long-term personal collection or loans for scanning, and emphasizes that the static site is designed to be cloneable. Canonical page: <https://bitsavers.org/>. Rights and access conditions vary by document.

### Retrocomputing Archive

The Retrocomputing Archive describes itself as an extension of the Commercial CP/M Software Archive, expanded to classic computer systems and their software from 6502-based single-board computers through VAX minicomputers. Its published inclusion rule is at least fifteen years old, which it identifies as including material before 2007. Canonical page: <http://www.retroarchive.org/>. The archive notes that submissions are judged individually and asks visitors to report broken links; rights and redistribution terms vary by item.
